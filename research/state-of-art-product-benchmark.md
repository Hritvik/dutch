# Dutch product benchmark against current shared-expense practice

## Scope and method

This is a product-decision benchmark, not a feature-count comparison. It compares the settled Dutch MVP behavior in [the capability handoff](../docs/product/dutch-mvp-capability.md) with publicly documented behavior from current products—**Splitwise**, **Tricount**, **Settle Up**, and selectively **Splitser**—and with official Apple/Android mobile-platform guidance where the decision is primarily mobile-system behavior. These are primary sources: vendor help/product/API documentation and platform documentation. Absence of a feature below means it was not established from a first-party source, not that no competitor offers it.

## Executive assessment

Dutch is aligned with the category's established mental model—ongoing Groups, lightweight non-group expenses, flexible splits, external payment recording, and debt simplification. It deliberately goes beyond typical consumer-product documentation in three valuable places: multi-payer accounting, explainable immutable financial history, and explicit multi-scope settlement allocations. Those choices make it technically safer and more auditable, but they add concepts that must remain mostly invisible during ordinary expense entry.

The strongest product risk is not correctness; it is **settlement comprehension**. Splitwise's own help centre says cross-scope “settle all” entries can be difficult to follow and says its automatic balancing entries do not automatically update when the originating payment is edited or deleted. Dutch's linked Allocation model is a meaningful improvement only if the client shows a simple summary first and makes allocation detail optional.

## Confirmed strengths

1. **The product model matches the category before it extends it.** Groups for recurring contexts and non-group/direct expenses for one-offs are proven, legible concepts.
2. **Dutch handles real-world accounting cases more faithfully.** Multi-payer contributions, deterministic rounding, and an immutable ledger remove ambiguity that consumer apps often leave implicit.
3. **Simplification is derived, not destructive.** This preserves the proven benefit of fewer payments while retaining a defensible explanation of where balances came from.
4. **The explicit allocation design is a real response to a documented market failure.** Splitwise's own help material acknowledges that automatic cross-scope settling can be hard to follow; Dutch can make it inspectable.
5. **The mobile trust model is current.** Contextual notification consent, collapsed alerts, best-effort delivery, local cached reads, and server-authoritative financial commands follow platform guidance.

## Gaps and trade-offs to manage deliberately

1. **Multi-payer is powerful but non-default.** It should not turn the common “one person paid dinner” flow into an accounting form. Use a one-payer default, obvious totals, and an advanced expansion.
2. **“Settle all” needs an explanation layer.** The product should show the net cash payment and selected scopes before confirmation, then a single parent confirmation with optional per-scope detail. Do not present synthetic-looking expense rows as if users created them individually.
3. **Open editing needs social safety, not just an audit trail.** Activity and immutable revisions support accountability, but edit/delete actions should name the affected scope and make a destructive correction explicit. Consider a lightweight undo window only if it creates a new correction rather than mutating history.
4. **Direct multi-person sharing needs careful language.** “Direct” can sound two-person. In UI, call the action “one-off split” or use the participant names, while reserving “Group” for continuing workspaces.
5. **The fixed Group currency is a deliberate MVP constraint.** It is more comprehensible than mixed-currency debt, but a group-creation warning should prevent a user selecting the wrong currency and discovering the lock after transactions exist.
6. **Offline clarity must be visually unmissable.** The platform supports local-first reads, but Dutch should use a persistent freshness label on balance-bearing screens and never let a local draft look recorded.

## Recommendations (without changing settled decisions)

1. Make the standard add-expense path: **amount → one payer → equal split → save**. Reveal additional payers and advanced split types behind intentional controls, while keeping live “paid” and “shared” totals visible.
2. Make Settlement scope selection a first-class precondition. The confirmation should state both the selected scope(s) and the before/after balance effect, including a reversal warning.
3. Design `Settle all` as a two-level explanation: a human sentence (“You paid ₹1,500; 3 balances are now settled”) and a drill-in allocation list. Preserve the parent/allocation Activity relationship in every detail view.
4. Keep Group simplified recommendations visually distinct from recorded payments—for example, “Suggested transfers” versus “Recorded settlements.”
5. Use Activity as the default answer to “why did my balance change?” and keep immutable revision/ledger terminology out of the primary UI; expose it only in an evidence detail.
6. Test comprehension with representative groups before implementation hardens: a normal one-payer dinner, a two-payer trip expense, an overpayment, a Group simplification, and a cross-scope settle-all. Success means users can predict what changes where, not merely complete the flow.
